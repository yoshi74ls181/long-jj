Line profile

The equation being solved

φtt + α φt = Z ∂x(Z−1 ∂xφ) − ωp2 sin φ + γ Z

Position is in Josephson lengths λJ0 and time in units of 1/ωp0, both at the reference values Z0 and ωp0. Z(x) and ωp(x) are the local line impedance and plasma frequency relative to that reference. The Swihart velocity c̄ stays uniform, so the local Josephson length is c̄/ωp. The energy is in units of E0, the Josephson energy stored in one λJ0 of the reference line, so a fluxon at rest on it has energy 8. A fluxon's rest energy scales as 8ωp/Z: fluxons are pushed towards low plasma frequency and high impedance, and an impedance step reflects plasma waves.

The line is discretized at λJ0/20 and stepped with a time step of 0.02/ωp0; the shunt loss is treated implicitly.

What each end does

  • Open (applied field). The bare edge of a junction. No current leaves the electrodes, and the field at the edge is held at the applied value φx = H, which is how a magnetic field enters a long junction.
  • Short circuit. The two electrodes are joined at the end by a superconducting short, so the voltage there is zero and the phase at the end is held at a whole multiple of 2π.
  • Periodic (ring). The line closes on itself, as in an annular junction. Fluxons placed on the ring are trapped and counted.
  • Absorbing (infinite line). A hidden buffer, 30 λJ0 long, sits beyond the window. In it the plasma frequency falls smoothly to zero, so a fluxon that enters spreads into a plain 2π phase step, and the buffer ends in a matched resistor, R = Z, that absorbs the step and any waves. Waves and fluxons that leave do not come back, as if the line went on forever, however many leave.
